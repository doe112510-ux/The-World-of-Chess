How to Play

Chess involves a lot of strategy, which can be intimidating for people trying to learn. But the learning curve with chess is not as steep as some people think. While learning the more complex strategies to become a master can be difficult, if your just looking to play chess for fun with friends, all you really need to know is how to use the pieces properly, and you can form your own strategies, as every game of chess is unique and the moves of the players can force people to think in the context of that specific game.

It is important to know how to use the pieces together and how they function on their own. Both teams have 16 pieces: 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, a queen and a king.

The pawns are the weakest units on the board, they can only move one space in front of them, but two spaces if its their first movement of the game. They can attack diagonally and can only moving to the space in front of them. If they reach the end of the board, it will be traded for a higher value piece that the player has lost. This can be a bishop, knight, rook, or a queen.

Rooks are the pieces that look like mini castle towers. Each team has two rooks that start the game on the corners. They move in straight lines in any direction, and can move from one side of the board to the other as long as there are no pieces in it's way. Rooks can do a special move with their king called castling, where the rook and king can switch places. Learn more at strategies. Rooks are considered a major piece and is worth 5 points. The only piece more valuable is the queen

Knights are the pieces that look like horses. Their starting location is in between the rook and the bishop at both ends of the board. Knights have the most unique way of moving out of all the pieces. Knights move in an l shape over 3 spaces in any direction, but they can move either vertically two squares and horizontally one space, or one space vertically and two spaces horizontally. If the knight you want to move is on space 6c and you want to move it to 8d, you can move it up two and right one or up one and right two. Either way, it will end up at the same space. Another unique thing about knights is that they are the only piece in the game that can jump over another piece A knight is considered a minor piece like a bishop and is worth 3 points.

Bishops are the tall and thin pieces with a large top and a small ball on the top of it's head. Their starting location is next to the knights, and the left bishop is next to the queen while the right one is next to the king. Bishops move in diagnol lines and as can move from one corner to the other as long as no pieces are in their way. They are the only piece in the game that is stuck to one tile color. The bishop that starts on a white tile cannot move to a black tile under any circumstance, and vice versa for the bishop that starts on a black tile. The bishop is considered a minor piece like a knight, much more valuable than a pawn but less valuable than a rook or a queen.

Queens are the most powerful pieces in chess. They are tall and the top looks like a crown. Their starting location is right next to the king behind the pawns. The queen can move in any way it wants. With the exception of the knight which moves in an L shape and can jump over other pieces, the queen can move like every other piece in the game combined. The can move straight forward, backward, sideways and diagnoally, and has no restrictions on how far it can move. If it is on tile 1A, it can move to any corner and in tile thats in between

The King is the most important piece in Chess. There is one King per team, and their starting location is behind the pawns, and in between the Queen and a Bishop. It is not a powerful piece, it can move in any direction, but only one tile at a time. The King must be protected at all costs, because killing the other teams king is the only way to win. If a King is under the threat of being eliminated, the term is called being in check. The king must be moved to safety immediatly. If there is no place the king can move to without being taken, then it is in checkmate and the game is over. A king cannot move into a position that puts it into check, and if it is in check the next move must break the check, either by moving the king, moving another piece to block the path, or by killing the piece that threatens the king
