Strategies

Chess is a game of pure strategy, and there are hundreds of strategies you can use to gain an advantage against your opponent. It is important to know how to use the pieces together and how they function on their own. Both teams have 16 pieces: 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, a queen and a king.

In a chess game, it is important to have a plan and have the pieces work together. The pawns are the most common and least valuable piece, so they can be a good standard piece to move forward first. You can use them to secure the center of the board, because whoever controls the center controls how far pieces and move and keep the other players pieces tied down. The pawns can also be great ways to lure the other player into a trap. If you place a pawn in a vunerable spot, the other player might take width a valuable piece, and once they make their move, you can take an important piece while only giving up a minor piece.

In a games, one thing you should always do is take control of the center of the board. If you control the center spaces, you can go on the offensive and control what moves the other player can make. If a piece is located on a center tile, even if it's not the very center, it has much more freedom of movement. A king or queen can move in 8 directions, a king moving one space while a queen can move as far as she wants if there are no obstacles in the way. If they are on the edge of the board, the number of directions they can move in is limited to 5, and if their in a corner, they can only move 3 spaces making them very easy prey. A good way to take control early on is with pawns. Pawns can move 2 spaces forward as their first move, so move the center pieces forward so you will have control and keep the other player on the defensive.

It is very important to keep the King as secure as possible, because if you lose the King you lost the game. While the king might be the most important, he is one of the weakest pieces in the game, so you have to use your other pieces to keep him safe. The kings starting position is in the center of the board at the very back behind the pawns and in between the Queen and a bishop, so it starts out in a very protected position. Since it is safe where it starts, it would be wise to not move the king until later in the game or if you have no choice. It would also be best to not move the pawns that are in front of him because the enemy queen and bishops will be able to put your king into checkmate, which will end the game in a loss for you.
